# Personal website brief: editorial maker learning in public

## Status and scope

This brief supersedes the narrower visual-refinement brief. It defines the
positioning, content model, visual direction, and prototyping process for
Israel Gutiérrez's personal website. It does not authorize implementation.
The visual direction must first be explored and approved through disposable
desktop and mobile prototypes using real content and imagery.

## Intent

Create a durable public home for Israel's professional identity: a senior
product leader in adult learning and edtech who still goes into product detail
and builds working solutions, increasingly with AI support.

The site is not an active job-search page. Its purpose is to establish a
coherent reputation now so that, if Israel looks for an international Senior
Product Manager or Product Director role in the future, his work, judgment,
and point of view are already visible and credible.

The working positioning is:

> A product leader who still builds.

The working editorial principle is:

> Serious about learning, not solemn about it.

These lines are strategic anchors, not necessarily final homepage copy.

## Primary audience

The primary visitor is someone hiring a senior product leader for an
international edtech or adult-learning company where English is the working
language. They should quickly understand that Israel can:

- Lead product direction and collaborate closely with engineering.
- Move between strategy, research, product detail, data, and implementation.
- Apply deep experience in adult learning and edtech.
- Use AI as a practical building tool rather than a fashionable identity.
- Communicate difficult ideas clearly and bring intelligence, curiosity, and
  humour to the work.

Secondary audiences are product and engineering peers, conference organisers,
and people who encounter Israel's projects or writing.

## Desired impression

Within roughly ten seconds, the site should communicate three qualities:

1. Professional and experienced.
2. Intelligent and playful rather than corporate or solemn.
3. Deeply interested in education, especially adult learning and its social
   impact.

Professionalism is the foundation, learning is the narrative thread, and
humour is a reward for attention rather than a constant performance.

## Goals

- Present Israel as a credible Senior PM / Product Director candidate for a
  future international edtech role without implying that he is currently
  looking for work.
- Make the hands-on maker dimension concrete through selected products,
  decisions, experiments, and working outputs.
- Show that AI expands Israel's ability to explore and implement product ideas;
  do not make AI the subject of the whole identity.
- Give adult learning and edtech more narrative weight than a conventional
  reverse-chronological career summary would.
- Use real visual evidence—project imagery and conference photography—to make
  the site human and memorable.
- Support lightweight monthly publishing in English through short notes.
- Keep LinkedIn, GitHub, and email easy to find as natural professional contact
  routes.
- Continue to work when publishing becomes less frequent than intended.

## Non-goals

- The site is not a consultancy or lead-generation website.
- It does not advertise services, solicit clients, or invite visitors to book a
  sales call.
- It does not say that Israel is open to work or actively seeking a role.
- It is not a complete CV, exhaustive timeline, or wall of credentials.
- It is not a showcase of AI aesthetics or a claim that AI is Israel's main
  professional identity.
- It is not designed around children, schools, exams, grades, or other visual
  shorthand that infantilises education; Israel's focus is adult learning.
- It does not promise a public publishing cadence.

## Anti-goals

The result should not contain or evoke:

- A corporate portrait with crossed arms or an executive stock-photo pose.
- Walls of text or long undifferentiated biography sections.
- Grandiose claims, exaggerated personal branding, or language that tries to
  sell something.
- Generic AI imagery such as purple gradients, particles, digital brains, or
  floating prompts.
- Childlike educational motifs such as pencils, chalkboards, light bulbs, or
  classroom illustrations.
- Constant animation, a novelty cursor, or playfulness that makes the site feel
  less senior.
- A wall of company logos that substitutes borrowed prestige for Israel's own
  story.
- Artificially perfect case studies that hide uncertainty, difficult choices,
  and learning.
- Memes, explained jokes, or a pun in every section.

## Selected approach

Three approaches were considered:

### Editorial maker

A calm editorial biography with strong typography, a natural portrait,
selected work, conference imagery, concise notes, and one or two meaningful
playful interactions. It offers the best balance of seniority and personality,
but can feel too restrained if it lacks real project evidence.

### Learning in public

A living field notebook organised around questions, experiments, prototypes,
and reflections. It makes curiosity and ongoing construction visible, but
depends more heavily on continued publishing and can obscure seniority.

### Product casebook

A project-led portfolio built around detailed cases, outcomes, and individual
contribution. It offers strong hiring evidence, but risks looking like an
active application or consultant portfolio and demands more publishable
project material.

### Chosen combination

Use **Editorial maker** as the durable frame, with a meaningful **Learning in
public** layer and selected evidence from the **Product casebook** approach.
The stable biography, work, and talks make the site complete at all times;
notes and experiments let it become richer as Israel publishes.

## Narrative and content hierarchy

### Opening

The first screen should establish Israel's identity as a hands-on product
leader, connect product and engineering, and make adult learning / edtech
visible early. Gobierto should establish the current role, but should not
define the whole identity or displace the longer learning-product story.

The introduction should be concise enough to scan. It should not read like a
cover letter or announce availability.

### Selected work

Show a small number of consequential chapters rather than every role. Each
chapter should answer, in compact form:

- What problem or domain was involved?
- What did Israel lead or build?
- What changed or what was learned?

Relevant chapters include current work at Gobierto, Leap by Microverse, data
and learning products, and earlier research / engineering. Results should be
used when verified and contextualised rather than displayed as a metric wall.

### Projects and experiments

Include a small set of things Israel has built or is actively exploring.
Projects may include a representative image, a compact description, the
question being explored, and links to the live result or source when public.

**Critical Reader** should launch as both:

- The first highlighted independent project.
- The first learning note, explaining the problem, what was built, how AI was
  used, and what Israel learned.

The project presentation and the note may share source material, but should not
duplicate the same text verbatim.

Critical Reader is an existing working Chrome-extension beta, not a conceptual
placeholder. It helps readers notice clearly discriminatory expressions in
Spanish-language articles by highlighting relevant paragraphs and explaining
the category when the reader explicitly asks it to analyse a page. Its purpose
is to encourage slower, more critical reading rather than decide what the
reader should think, judge an author, or label a publication.

The project is especially relevant to Israel's story because it began from a
personal learning need, became a functioning product through hands-on work
with AI support, and sits at the intersection of adult learning, critical
thinking, product judgment, engineering, privacy, and responsible AI. The
source project is `gootyfer/critical-reader`. Existing usable visual material
includes its blue-and-yellow highlighter icon and a real 1280×800 product
screenshot in the project's extension store assets. Copy those assets into
the website only after confirming they are the intended public versions; do
not depend on paths inside another temporary worktree.

### Talks

Talks should provide visual, human proof that Israel can communicate and engage
an audience. Use real conference photographs or event imagery where rights and
quality permit. Prioritise recent and representative talks, with title, event,
place, year, and a link.

Talk imagery should contribute energy and personality rather than create a
generic speaker reel.

Israel expects to have personal photographs from past talks but needs to
locate them. Their absence does not block brief approval. Before the first
high-fidelity visual prototype, assemble a small approved asset set with at
least one talk photograph, its event and year, photographer credit when known,
and confirmation that it may be published. If no suitable image is available,
prototype the talk section with clearly labelled temporary event imagery and
do not treat that prototype as final approval for production assets.

### Notes

Use one editorial archive called **Notes** rather than separate Blog, Lab, and
Writing products. Notes can include:

- Built experiments and what they taught.
- Transferable lessons from day-to-day product work at Gobierto, written
  without confidential information.
- Reflections on adult learning, edtech, product leadership, and practical AI.
- Occasional talk notes or visual explanations.

The intended rhythm is approximately monthly, but the site must not state or
depend on that promise. A typical note should be approachable to write and read:
roughly 500–1,000 words and focused on one useful idea. The homepage should
feature only a few recent or selected notes; the archive holds the rest.

Approved public themes from current work at Gobierto include how Israel makes
prototypes, how the product-development cycle works, how AI supports different
parts of that process, practical techniques that have worked for him, and what
he learns while helping prepare a course for public-procurement bidders. These
notes should discuss transferable process and learning without revealing the
specific confidential feature currently being built, private customer data,
internal strategy, or unpublished commercial information.

### Contact

Keep LinkedIn, GitHub, and email visible. Contact language should invite normal
professional conversation about product, learning, or building, not projects
for hire or employment offers.

## Voice and language

- English is the primary language for the interface and notes.
- A Spanish note is acceptable when the topic or audience makes Spanish the
  natural choice; the entire site does not need to be duplicated.
- The voice is clear, thoughtful, direct, and human.
- Prefer concrete observations and specific language to leadership jargon.
- Use intelligent wordplay sparingly, especially where it connects learning,
  building, questioning, or shipping.
- The author should sound like a curious practitioner, not an infallible expert
  delivering lessons from above.

## Visual direction

### Overall character

Build on the warm, minimal editorial direction already accepted, but make it
more visual and lived-in. The page should feel like an independent publication
or thoughtful maker's profile, not a SaaS landing page, executive CV, or design
template.

Use strong hierarchy, short text blocks, generous but not excessive space,
editorial rules, and a restrained palette. Avoid grids of interchangeable
cards.

### Portrait

- Use a natural, approachable photograph rather than a corporate pose.
- Preserve the source image's real aspect ratio in prototypes and production.
- Do not stretch the image or crop the face without explicit visual approval.
- Test the portrait at desktop and mobile with the real asset; placeholder
  imagery is not sufficient for approval.
- The portrait supports identity but must not dominate the first screen.

### Project and talk imagery

- Prefer real screenshots, artefacts, and event photographs over illustrations
  generated for decoration.
- Every image should prove something: a product exists, an idea was explored,
  or Israel was present and communicating.
- Use captions when context is not obvious.
- Maintain consistent colour treatment or framing without forcing every source
  into the same crop.

### Company and event logos

Logos may appear only when they help identify a specific project, employer, or
event. Keep them small and contextual. Do not create a standalone logo wall.

### Motion and play

Include one memorable interaction or short motion idea connected to learning
or building. It should reward attention and reveal meaning—for example, a
phrase that changes through exploration or a rigid idea that can be taken
apart and reconstructed. This example is not a final animation specification.

Motion must:

- Never block reading, navigation, or contact.
- Be subtle by default and stop rather than loop continuously.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Avoid generic AI particles, gratuitous parallax, or novelty cursor effects.

Final motion concepts are left to the visual-prototype phase.

## Information architecture

The default direction is a simple structure:

- **Home** — identity, selected work, highlighted project, selected talks,
  latest notes, contact.
- **Notes** — chronological archive with lightweight type/topic labels.
- **Note detail** — focused reading page, with space for images and code or
  diagrams when useful.
- **Project detail or external link** — only when a project needs more than the
  compact homepage treatment; do not require a detail page for every project.

Navigation should remain simple. LinkedIn, GitHub, and email may be persistent
utility links rather than full navigation destinations.

## Resilience and maintenance

- The homepage must still feel current if the latest note is several months
  old.
- Prefer selected work and durable statements over widgets that expose
  inactivity.
- Publishing a note should require editing one Markdown/MDX content file and
  adding optional local media, without touching layout code.
- Do not require a CMS, account, database, or newsletter system for the first
  release.
- Preserve RSS support for readers who want to follow notes.

## Accessibility and performance

- Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic structure, sufficient
  contrast, alternative text, and reduced motion.
- Images must be responsive and dimensioned to avoid layout shift.
- Avoid heavy animation libraries unless the approved motion prototype cannot
  be implemented responsibly without one.
- The site must remain fast on mobile and functional without JavaScript except
  for the optional playful interaction.

## Visual prototyping before implementation

Implementation is gated on visual approval. The prototype phase must show:

1. A desktop homepage using real copy, the real portrait, Critical Reader
   material, and at least one real talk image.
2. A mobile homepage demonstrating portrait treatment, content order, and
   navigation.
3. A note detail page showing the intended reading experience.
4. At least two treatments for the meaningful playful interaction, including
   reduced-motion behaviour.

The prototypes may be disposable screenshots or interactive spikes. They are
for deciding composition, imagery, tone, and motion, not production code.

## Existing technical context

The current implementation uses Astro, content collections, Markdown notes,
RSS, responsive images, and GitHub Pages deployment. Unless prototyping reveals
a material limitation, implementation should evolve this foundation rather
than restart with a new stack.

Historical routes, metadata, accessibility checks, cross-browser coverage, and
the eventual `israelgutierrez.es` custom-domain setup remain production
requirements, but they do not drive the visual brief.

The required historical route set is already defined by the existing site and
tests: preserve the archived Spanish article at
`/blog/2014/11/03/hackea-tu-educacion-durante-la-universidad/`, plus usable
compatibility pages at `/blog/` and `/blog/archives/`. The archived 2014 article
must remain reachable but should not be mixed into current Notes or presented
as Israel's current thinking.

## Decisions left to the designer / builder

- Final homepage wording within the approved positioning and verified facts.
- Exact typefaces, scale, palette refinement, grid, and spacing.
- The number and crop of talk images after source quality and rights are known.
- Whether individual projects need internal detail pages or link externally.
- The specific non-looping interaction proposed during prototyping.
- Minor responsive composition decisions that preserve the approved hierarchy.

## Approval gates

1. Approve this brief.
2. Produce and iterate disposable visual prototypes.
3. Approve one visual direction and motion treatment.
4. Write an implementation plan.
5. Implement and verify.
